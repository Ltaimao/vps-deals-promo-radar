How to Move Your Site to a Cheap VPS Without Downtime: A Checklist Guide

Short answer: lower your DNS TTL 48 hours before, copy everything to the new VPS, test the site on the new IP, switch email first, flip DNS, then keep the old host for a week. Nothing on that list costs money. The one thing that costs money is the overlap — one extra month of hosting — and at the budget tier that is roughly the price of a coffee. Here is the checklist in order:

StepWhat to doWhy it matters
1. Size the destinationMatch the new VPS to the old host's RAM and disk — not the CPU coresMemory, not CPU, is what kills budget moves; 2 GB runs 3–6 WordPress sites with caching
2. Take a backup you can restoreFiles + database, and verify it restores somewhereA backup you have never restored is a rumor, not a backup
3. Lower DNS TTL to 600 seconds, 48 hours earlyDrop the TTL before the move, not duringCuts worst-case propagation from 24 hours to ~10 minutes
4. Copy, then test on the new IPrsync or a snapshot, then load the site from the new addressDNS is the last thing you flip, never the first
5. Move email before the DNS flipPoint MX to the new mailbox setup firstA raw VPS has no mailbox — miss this and you lose mail silently
6. Flip DNS, then keep the old host for 7 daysDo not cancel anything on moving dayThe overlap month is the cheapest insurance on the page

The data behind this guide: a snapshot of 222 live VPS price cards across 28 providers, taken 2026-10-22. Every price below comes from that snapshot; every step comes from publicly documented migration practice. Nothing is guessed.

The one number that decides everything: 222 price cards, 1 mentions migration

We searched all 222 card titles for migration mentions. The result: exactly one card — "Free migrations with supported control panel Starting at" $43.25 per month (KnownHost, 2026-10-22 snapshot). That is a managed plan priced at 8.65× the median entry price of $5.00/month across the 24 providers in our snapshot that have a USD monthly tier.

Read that finding as the market telling you its price: at the budget tier, migration is a DIY job. The cheapest managed migration costs more than most people pay for eight months of hosting. The budget alternative is not a worse migration — it is the six steps above, done in order. Meanwhile the only card mentioning WordPress is an enterprise cloud plan at $6.99/month — the budget tier does not sell you a migration service, so buy the checklist instead.

The math of the overlap month

The fear that drives bad moves is paying twice. So here is the actual number. The median entry plan in our snapshot is $5.00/month; 14 of 24 providers start at $5 or less, 7 at $3 or less, and 3 under $2 (Hostens $1.00, Latitude.sh $1.23, BuyVM $1.25). Running the old host and the new VPS side by side for one month therefore costs roughly $5 to $10 total — less than a single hour of paid migration help.

Compare that to the cost of skipping the overlap: cancelling the old host on moving day, finding out at 2 a.m. that the database import truncated a table, and having no source left to re-copy from. The overlap month is not waste; it is a $5 insurance policy with a 100% payout rate on the only failure mode that cannot be undone.

TTL: the one DNS number worth knowing

DNS propagation is not a mystery; it is one number — the TTL (time to live) on your records. A typical host sets TTL to 86,400 seconds (24 hours). If you flip DNS with that TTL, some visitors keep landing on the old server for a full day.

TTLWhat it meansWorst-case propagation
86,400 s (default)Cached for 24 hoursUp to 24 hours
600 s (lowered)Cached for 10 minutes~10 minutes

Lower the TTL to 600 at least 48 hours before the move — two full days lets the old 86,400-second value expire everywhere. Then flip DNS and raise the TTL back afterwards. Skipping this step is the single most common cause of "it works for me but not for my customers."

Email moves first, DNS moves last

A raw budget VPS has no mailbox. That is worth saying twice, because "Does a VPS include email for WordPress?" is one of the most common real questions people ask — and the answer is usually no on unmanaged plans. If you flip DNS before mail is working, messages sent to your domain quietly die in transit.

Move the mailboxes first — Google Workspace, Fastmail, or a dedicated mail provider — and confirm you can send and receive from the new setup before the DNS flip. Mail is the part of the move with no undo button.

Should you just stay on managed WordPress instead?

That is the honest alternative, and it deserves a number. Publicly reported 2026 pricing puts a managed layer at roughly $54/month for a 4 GB equivalent versus $24/month for a raw 4 GB VPS on the same infrastructure — a $30/month premium for staging, support, and not touching a terminal. Our snapshot's whole budget floor sits far below either number: 14 providers start at $5 or less.

Buy the premium if the site earns money and your time is billable — a managed plan with free migration and support is the right trade. Buy the raw VPS if the site is a hobby, a test, or a learning box — and run the checklist above. The wrong choice is the middle one: a raw VPS with no checklist and no overlap month.

Our take: the move is six steps, and step 6 is the whole thing

If we were moving a site tomorrow we would do all six steps, but if we could only afford one piece of advice it would be step 6: keep the old server for a week. Everything else — TTL, test copies, email order — reduces risk. The overlap eliminates the one risk that money cannot fix after the fact: having nowhere to go back to. At a median entry price of $5.00/month, a week of overlap costs about a dollar. There is no cheaper insurance in hosting.
