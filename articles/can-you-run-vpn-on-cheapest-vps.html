Q1: Will the cheapest plan actually run WireGuard or OpenVPN?

Yes. A VPN endpoint is one of the lightest workloads you can put on a server: it encrypts packets and forwards them. WireGuard in particular is famously frugal — it runs comfortably on boxes with a few hundred megabytes of RAM, far below what even the cheapest VPS tiers ship. OpenVPN is heavier but still trivial for any 1 GB plan. For a VPN, bandwidth policy matters more than CPU or RAM, and no provider in our table sells a plan too small to run WireGuard.

Q2: What does a self-hosted VPN cost versus a commercial VPN?

Our data says $1–$5/month for the server itself. A worked example from ishosting's 2026 comparison puts a self-hosted WireGuard box at about $5.94/month on annual billing, versus roughly $92/year for a dedicated-IP add-on from a commercial VPN provider (source). The honest trade: a commercial VPN subscription is simpler (apps, many locations, no maintenance); a $2–$5 VPS gives you one clean IP you fully control and can also host other things on the same box. If all you want is encrypted café Wi-Fi, the cheapest subscription usually wins on convenience.

Q3: What's the catch with the $1–$2/month entries?

Three things to check before you celebrate:

  1. Billing cadence. The cheapest row in our table, Hostens at $1.00, lists daily/weekly billing — fine for testing, awkward as a permanent setup. And as our hourly-pricing guide showed, sub-dollar entries are usually hourly rates that cap at the monthly price.
  2. Bandwidth policy. VPN traffic is pure bandwidth. Our tracker captures prices, not bandwidth caps or overage fees — a provider page may advertise "unmetered" with fair-use limits buried in the terms. We don't track those; read the terms page for the plan you're buying.
  3. Terms of service. A few providers restrict VPN or proxy use (or restrict it on the cheapest tiers). Our price scraper doesn't read ToS pages, so this one needs a manual check — search the provider's terms for "VPN" before you buy.

Q4: Does the box need IPv4, or is IPv6-only fine?

WireGuard itself works on both, but in practice you want an IPv4 address: most of the networks and services you'll reach from a personal VPN are still IPv4. Some of the very cheapest tiers in the market are IPv6-only. We say this plainly because we can't verify it from our data — our tracker doesn't capture IPv4/IPv6 support per plan. If the plan page doesn't say "IPv4 included," ask support before buying.

Q5: So which plan should I actually pick?

Our take: skip the $1 curiosities unless you like tinkering, and pick a monthly-billed entry in the $2–$5 range from the table above — IONOS, Virmach, or RackNerd at the bottom end; DigitalOcean, Kamatera, or RamNode at $4 if you want a mainstream cloud. That's the sweet spot where the billing is boring, the IP is IPv4, and the box is yours to keep.
