Q: Why is one provider so much cheaper than another for similar specs?

This is the question Reddit asks monthly about the cheapest European hosts, and the commonly reported answer has five parts: hardware bought in bulk, datacenters the provider owns rather than rents, CPU cores shared across many tenants at ratios that work for bursty workloads, unmanaged service so there is no support staff cost to pass on, and flat monthly or annual billing with no hourly metering to operate. Nothing exotic — just fewer layers of cost between the metal and your invoice.

Q: Is the ultra-low price real, or will it jump on renewal?

It is usually real. The cheapest annual specials in our data are priced-to-stay, not bait-and-switch — the catch is the commitment: you pay 12 months up front and stock per location is limited. Where prices do jump is the classic intro-rate model, which we covered in full in our Q&A on renewals and hidden fees. Rule of thumb: a $15/year special that renews at $15/year is a commitment play; a $1.99 first-month rate with no renewal price printed next to it is a pricing play. Read the cart page, not the banner.

Q: Will my site be slow because the VPS is cheap?

It depends on your workload, not the price tag. A static site, a blog, or a WireGuard VPN barely touches the CPU — those run fine on a $1–$2 tier. The moment you run heavy database joins or a plugin-loaded CMS on a single shared core, you will feel it. That is physics, not a defect: shared cores mean your neighbors' load is part of your performance envelope. We do not run our own benchmarks (we will not invent numbers), so our advice is workload-first — see which VPS for which job for the scenario picks.

Q: What do I actually give up on the cheapest tier?

The checklist at the top, in practice:

  • Backups are on you. Most budget tiers either charge extra for automated backups or do not offer them. Set up your own off-site copy on day one.
  • Support is tickets, sometimes slow. Fine for "my invoice is wrong", less fine for "my server is down at 3 a.m.".
  • Fewer locations. Budget hosts concentrate in a few datacenters, so check that one is near your visitors before you buy.
  • No hand-holding. Unmanaged means the OS install, the firewall, and the 2 a.m. outage are all yours.

Q: When is an ultra-cheap VPS the right buy?

Buy the $1–$5 tier when the job is boring: a static site, a personal blog, a staging box, a VPN endpoint, a cron runner. Spend more when the job is revenue: a store taking card payments, anything with an SLA you promised someone, or a workload you cannot afford to debug at midnight. And if a plan looks impossibly cheap, run it through our renewal checklist and the hourly-vs-monthly math before you enter your card — the price on the banner is only the first of three numbers that matter.
