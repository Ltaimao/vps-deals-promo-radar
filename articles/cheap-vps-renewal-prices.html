Do Cheap VPS Prices Jump on Renewal? A Q&A on Renewals and Hidden Fees

The price on the provider's homepage is the price of the first term. What you pay in month two, or year two, can be a different number — and the roundups we audited never tell you which is which. They write "higher renewal pricing" in the cons column and move on, without a single renewal figure you can check.

This Q&A does the checkable thing instead. Every price figure below comes from this site's own scrape — 230 live offers across the 28 providers we list, last verified 2026-10-02 — and where our data can't answer a question, we say so instead of guessing.

1. Does the price renew higher next month?

This is the most-asked cheap-VPS pricing question we see, and here is the honest, checkable answer: the renewal rate is almost never printed next to the price you click. In our 2026-10-02 snapshot, exactly zero of 230 live offers carried an expiry date or a stated renewal rate. The number you see is the first-term price; the number you will pay later lives on a different page — the terms of service, the FAQ, or the cart's fine print — if it is published at all.

That does not mean every provider raises prices. Many budget VPS brands bill the same rate every month or every year, and simply renew at the list price. The trap is different: you cannot tell from the price page alone whether you are looking at an introductory rate or the standing rate. Before you pay, open the cart and read the renewal line item — the honest providers print it there.

2. Is the $11.29/year deal real, or does it jump on renewal?

Yes, the deal is real — for the first year. Annual "deal" pricing on budget VPS is typically a first-term price; year two bills at whatever the provider's standard annual rate is on that day. Whether that stings depends on one word in the terms: "recurring". If the terms say the price recurs, year two should match. If they say "promotional" or "first term", assume it will not.

Annual pricing is where the cheapest headline numbers in our data live — for example, RackNerd's entry offers from $2.00/month and Virmach's from $2.00/month in our 2026-10-02 snapshot. Divide any annual deal by 12 and ask: would I still buy this at that monthly figure if it recurs higher? If the answer is no, set a calendar reminder two weeks before renewal, not two weeks after.

3. What counts as a hidden fee on a cheap VPS?

"Hidden fee" usually means one of these line items that the headline price does not include. None of these are accusations against any specific provider — they are the places to check on the terms page before you pay:

  • Backups and snapshots. Automatic backups are the most commonly missing feature on budget tiers. Some providers charge per snapshot or per gigabyte; some offer none at all.
  • Bandwidth overage. Extra gigabytes can be billed, or the port can be throttled to a crawl. See question 5.
  • Extra IPv4 addresses. Almost always an add-on with a monthly fee.
  • Control-panel licenses. cPanel and similar panels are licensed per month, per server — the license can cost more than the VPS.
  • Setup fees. Rare on VPS, but they appear on some dedicated and legacy lines.
  • Migration assistance. Moving your data in is sometimes free, sometimes billed.
  • Taxes. VAT or sales tax is added at checkout in many regions and is never in the headline price.

A provider is not "scammy" for charging for these. The honest test is whether the fees are listed somewhere findable before checkout — a pricing page with an add-ons section passes; a fee you first meet on the invoice does not.

4. Are backups included, or do they cost extra?

"Are backups included with cheap VPS, or do they cost extra?" is a real community question, and the real answer is: usually not, on the cheapest tiers. Budget VPS is unmanaged by default — the provider keeps the node running, and your data is your job. Community wisdom on this is blunt: backups are on you.

Before you buy, check the plan's feature list for the words "backup" or "snapshot" and what they cost. If backups cost extra, budget for them like part of the server: a $2/month box with a paid backup add-on is a $4/month box. And whatever you choose, test a restore once — an untested backup is a rumor.

5. What happens when I exceed my bandwidth?

Another real question from deal-forum comment threads, and it has three possible answers depending on the provider: you get throttled to a slow port until the month resets, you get billed per extra gigabyte, or the service gets suspended until you upgrade. All three exist in the budget VPS market, and the plan page does not always say which one applies to you.

Two vocabulary traps to know. "Unmetered" is not "unlimited" — it means usage is not counted, but the port speed caps what you can physically push, and fair-use policies still apply. "1 TB bandwidth" means 1 TB of transfer, up and down combined on most providers — our earlier sizing guide walks through what that covers in practice. If your workload is bandwidth-heavy (video, proxies, game servers), the overage rule matters more than the headline price.

6. Can I get my money back if the box is bad?

"Is there a money-back guarantee?" comes up constantly in recommendation threads. The guarantee exists in some form at many providers, but the window is measured in days — commonly 7 to 30 — and it usually covers the first term only, not renewals. Read two lines before paying: how many days, and whether "any reason" or only specific failures qualify.

A guarantee is not a trial. If you want to test a provider risk-free, look for hourly or per-second billing instead — several providers in our snapshot bill that way, which means a bad box costs you cents to abandon, not a month.

7. How do I know if a provider just raised its prices?

You watch the list price over time — which is exactly what this site does. Our price history runs 38 snapshots back to 2026-09-23, and in that window 13 of 167 tracked price series moved. UpCloud's entry plans, for example, moved from €6 to €7 and €8 to €10 between snapshots. When a provider raises its list price, renewals usually follow the new list price, so a provider with a rising price history is a provider whose renewal you should double-check.

We do not yet track per-provider renewal rates — that is on our roadmap — but the list-price history is the next best signal, and it is public on our provider pages. If the renewal question matters to your budget, check the price history before the first payment, not after the second invoice.

The pre-payment checklist

Five minutes, before you type your card number:

  1. Open the cart and read the renewal line item, not just the headline price.
  2. Search the terms for "recurring" vs "promotional" / "first term".
  3. Check whether backups/snapshots are included or billed.
  4. Find the bandwidth overage rule: throttled, billed, or suspended.
  5. Note the refund window in days and what it covers.
  6. Add tax to the mental total — the headline price never includes it.
