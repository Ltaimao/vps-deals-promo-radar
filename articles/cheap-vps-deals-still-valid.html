Are Cheap VPS Deals Still Valid? A Date-First Q&A

A price without a date is a rumor. This Q&A answers the five questions readers actually ask about cheap VPS deal pages — using this site's own price database, where every figure carries the exact moment it was last verified.

Q1: Is this cheap VPS price still valid?

The 10-second check: look for a date on the page. If the page shows when the price was last checked — or when the offer expires — you have something to trust. If it doesn't, assume the number is stale until proven otherwise.

Q3: Why is the same VPS listed at $0.01 and $4?

Because a price without a billing unit is as useless as a price without a date. This morning's snapshot shows the trap clearly: DigitalOcean, Kamatera, CloudCone, and Liquid Web all have entries priced at $0.01–$0.03 — but the listing titles say "per-sec billing", "Hourly rate", and "Hourly Compute". Those are per-second or per-hour figures, not $0.01/month plans. A $4/month Droplet and a $0.01/hour listing can describe the same machine.

Before you compare two prices, confirm three things match:

  1. The billing unit — monthly, hourly, or per-second.
  2. The date — when each figure was last verified.
  3. The billing term — first-month promo vs. ongoing rate.

In our database, 47 of 224 entries price at or under $5 — but the sub-$1 ones are hourly-billed listings, not monthly plans. Strip those out and the real entry-level monthly band starts around $1–2/mo.

Q4: The post says "coupon: $2/mo" — is the coupon still good?

Probably unknowable from the post alone. Here is a telling number from our own feed: of the 224 offers we track across 31 providers, zero carry a provider-published expiry date. Providers simply don't ship machine-readable "valid until" fields on their pricing pages, so deal blogs can't show them either — they can only show the date they last verified the deal.

When a coupon's fate is unclear, run this checklist:

  1. Check the post's date or the site's "last verified" stamp.
  2. Click through to the provider's pricing or checkout page — dead coupons usually fail loudly there.
  3. On this site, use the deal card's fetched_at time: if we verified the price this morning and the provider page agrees, the deal is alive.

Q5: How fresh should deal data be?

Fresh enough that a price change can't hide between checks. VPS promos rotate constantly — especially around events like Black Friday — so we re-read all 31 provider pages every 6 hours and keep 22 snapshots going back to 2026-09-23. One bad scrape can't fake a trend when the next check lands six hours later.

What should you demand from any deal page? Three things: a visible verification date, a stated refresh cadence, and prices read from the provider's own page rather than copied from another roundup. Credit where it's due: of the three big roundups we audited, hostingadvice does date its list (marked updated 2026-09-25). The other two mostly don't — which is exactly the gap this article fills.
