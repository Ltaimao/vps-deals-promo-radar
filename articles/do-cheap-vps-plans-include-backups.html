Are Backups Included in Cheap VPS Plans? A Q&A on What Backup Really Costs

Short answer: almost never — budget for it separately. We scraped 214 live VPS offers across 32 providers on 2026-10-15. Only 9 of the 214 price cards mention backups at all, and only one of them — OVHcloud's, at $4.54/month — says "daily backup included" outright. The going rate when it is a paid add-on is about 20% of the plan price, confirmed by two independent public sources. The questions and answers below are the whole story.

On the other side, Hostinger's KVM VPS line includes free weekly backups, so inclusion is a feature of the plan, not of the price tier.

Q2: How much does the backup add-on cost when it is paid?

The market's going rate is 20% of the plan price. Two independent public sources land on the same number: a community Vultr deployment guide notes that enabling automatic backups "adds 20% to the base price", and Arct Cloud's public pricing (October 2026) says "Backups add 20% of the server's plan price". Q3: What is the difference between a backup and a snapshot?

Providers list them as separate features — CloudCone's cards say "Enable Auto-backups / Enable Snapshots" — because they solve different problems:

  • Snapshot: a point-in-time copy of the whole disk, usually manual, usually stored in the same datacenter. Great for "save before I break something", bad as your only copy — if the datacenter has a bad day, your snapshot is in it too.
  • Backup: a scheduled copy with retention (daily, 7 days, 30 days), ideally stored off-site. RackNerd's JetBackup for shared/reseller plans, for example, is replicated to off-site disaster-recovery datacenters and does not count against the plan's storage quota.

A snapshot is a parachute you packed this morning. A backup is a parachute packed by someone else and kept in a different building. You want at least one of the second kind.

Q4: Can I back up a cheap VPS myself for free?

Yes, and for hobby boxes this is the right answer. The community FAQ that calls backups "your responsibility" on budget VPS recommends BorgBackup or Restic paired with object storage — both are free, open-source tools that do encrypted, incremental backups. A weekly cron job pushing to any S3-compatible object storage covers a personal blog or test box for pennies a month.

The one rule that matters: a backup you have never restored from is a rumor, not a backup. Pick one unimportant weekend, delete a file, restore it, and time yourself. If the restore works, your backup is real. If it does not, you just learned that before it mattered.

Q5: So what should I actually do?

A three-way split that matches the price math above:

  • The site earns money: pay the add-on. On the median $4.54/month entry plan that is roughly $0.91/month — twenty cents a week to not lose the thing that pays you. This is not the line to optimize.
  • Hobby, learning, staging: DIY weekly with BorgBackup or Restic, plus one restore drill so you know the backup is real.
  • Budget unmanaged VPS (the RackNerd-type plan): assume nothing is included. If the plan page does not say the word "backup", you do not have one.

Our take: we pay the backup add-on on anything that earns money and do it ourselves for everything else. The median cheap VPS costs $4.54 a month and its backup costs about a dollar — the invoice is never what hurts. The hurt is the Sunday morning you discover there was nothing to restore.
