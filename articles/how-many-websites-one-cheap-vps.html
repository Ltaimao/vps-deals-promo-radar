How Many Websites Can One Cheap VPS Host? Real Prices, Per-Site Math

Short answer: as many as its RAM and CPU allow — no provider we track caps the number of domains per server. The practical ceiling is memory, not policy: a 2 GB VPS comfortably handles three to six small WordPress sites with caching. And the money math is what makes consolidation attractive. In our 2026-10-12 snapshot of 214 live offers across 26 providers, scraped from official pricing pages, the median cheapest entry plan costs $4.54/month. Put three sites on it and you pay about $1.51 per site per month; put five sites on it and you pay about $0.91 — instead of $22.70 for five separate entry servers.

The real limit is RAM, not domains

This is the part pricing pages never spell out. A provider's own public documentation states there is no restriction on the number of domains that can point at a single instance — the limit is the CPU and RAM you bought. So the question is never "am I allowed to," it is "can the box take it."

The community's rule of thumb is consistent across sources: a 2 GB VPS handles three to six active WordPress sites with caching, and a 2 vCPU / 4 GB box handles three to five small sites at moderate traffic. Those are not our measurements — they are widely repeated operator estimates — but they line up with what our price data makes possible: seven providers in our snapshot sell a monthly entry plan at or under $3 (Hostens from $1.00, IONOS from $2.00, RackNerd from $2.24), and fifteen providers have an entry plan at or under $5. The consolidation server itself is cheap; the scarce resource is memory.

The per-site math, from real prices

Start from the median: $4.54/month is the middle of what 23 providers charge for their cheapest monthly plan. At that price, every additional site you move onto the same box cuts the per-site cost without adding a cent to the bill — the server is already paid for. The second site halves your per-site cost; the fifth takes it under a dollar.

The honest version of the same table uses your own number instead of the median: take the entry price of the provider you actually buy (Hostens $1.00, IONOS $2.00, DigitalOcean $4.00 in our snapshot) and divide by the number of sites the box can comfortably run. A $2.00 IONOS box holding four small sites is $0.50 per site per month — cheaper than any per-site hosting product we have seen, because per-site products charge you for management, not for the server.

When one server stops being enough

Consolidation has a failure mode, and it is shared fate: one site's traffic spike slows every site on the box. A widely reused buyer's checklist phrases it as a question — is your traffic predictable? Steady, low-traffic sites consolidate beautifully; one spiky site argues for its own box, because a shared server has no way to contain a neighbor's bad day. The same logic applies to maintenance: one server means one update run, one backup to verify, one incident that takes everything down at once. Separate servers cost more precisely because they fail separately.

There is also a skills cost. Per-site managed hosting exists because someone has to do the sysadmin work: updates, SSL renewals, backups, watching the disk fill up. One published price comparison lists managed WordPress at $55.30/month for five sites — about $11 per site — against our $0.91 per site on a self-managed entry VPS. That $10-per-site gap is not a scam; it is the price of never touching a terminal. If you cannot or will not do the maintenance, the cheap math does not apply to you.

Our take: consolidate, but count the memory first

We would buy like this. For a handful of low-traffic sites — a blog, a portfolio, two client brochure sites — one $4-to-$5 entry VPS is the rational buy, and the per-site cost under a dollar is real as long as the sites stay small. The moment any single site can spike, give it its own box: shared fate is the tax consolidation charges, and it is collected exactly when you can least afford it. And if the words "SSH into the server and check the logs" sound like a chore rather than a Tuesday, pay for managed hosting instead — the $10-per-site premium is cheaper than the weekend you will spend learning server administration under pressure.
