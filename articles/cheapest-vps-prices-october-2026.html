Cheapest VPS Prices in October 2026: What 214 Live Offers Across 32 Providers Actually Show

Every number below comes from this site's own scrape — 214 live offers across 32 providers, snapshot taken 2026-10-18. Raw scraper output is noisy (hourly billing, storage add-ons, and price flips all look like "cheap VPS" to a dumb parser), so we filtered it down to 84 offers that are recognizable as monthly-billed VPS plans before computing anything. The full methodology — including all 130 excluded offers — is published at the bottom of this page.

The short answer

  • Cheapest verified monthly VPS: €1.67/mo (~$1.94) — AlphaVPS storage VPS. Five plans sit under $3/mo.
  • Windows VPS is nearly extinct at the budget end: 1 of 214 offers mentions Windows (AccuWebHosting, $6.33/mo).
  • 36.9% of real monthly plans cost under $10/mo (31 of 84).
  • 6 of 32 providers returned zero usable offers in this snapshot — their pricing pages yielded nothing our scraper could parse as a plan.

The sub-$10 segment (31 plans, 36.9%) is where the "cheap VPS" market actually lives. Above $20 you are mostly looking at managed plans, larger configurations, or providers whose entry tier simply isn't budget-oriented.

Windows VPS: one listing in the entire dataset

Of 214 offers, exactly one is explicitly a Windows VPS: AccuWebHosting's Windows VPS SSD plan at $6.33/mo. If you need Windows, the budget market is effectively a single-vendor market in this snapshot — a useful thing to know before you start comparing.

Providers with no usable offers this round

Six providers returned zero parseable offers: Hetzner Cloud, InterServer, ServerMania, HostHatch, A2 Hosting, and Bitlaunch. Two more were excluded entirely after filtering — Cherry Servers (5 offers, all add-ons like floating IPs and backup storage, no VPS plan) and Latitude.sh (12 offers, all title fragments or hourly-billed). "No offers" means our scraper couldn't parse a plan from their pricing page this round — not that they sell nothing.

Why we filtered out 130 of 214 offers

A scraper sees numbers; it doesn't know what they mean. Left unfiltered, the "cheapest VPS" in our data would be $0.01 — per-second billing noise from DigitalOcean and Kamatera. The 130 exclusions fall into six buckets:

  • Sub-$1.00 noise (19): per-second/hourly billing and storage add-ons priced per GB.
  • Known price flips (6): offers our history file shows bouncing between two prices every snapshot (DreamHost $39.99↔$59.99, SSDNodes $9.42↔$11.08, three UpCloud tiers) — scraper artifacts, not real prices.
  • Non-monthly billing (8): annual-only or hourly-only listings.
  • Not a VPS (18): block storage, dedicated servers, domains, GPUs, managed Kubernetes, web hosting.
  • Title fragments (26): scraped text too broken to map to any real plan (e.g. "Gbps Locations Starting").
  • Unattributable (53): the current price doesn't appear anywhere in that offer's own price history — usually several plans sharing one scraped title. We excluded rather than guessed.

Currency conversion (EUR→USD 1.16, GBP→USD 1.34) was used only for sorting and bucketing; original prices and currencies are listed as scraped. We deliberately publish no trend claims (no "prices rose X%"): our price history only goes back to 2026-09-23, and 76 of 214 offers can't be stably matched across snapshots yet. A trend computed on that would be fiction.

What this means if you're buying

  • Under $3/mo is real, but read what you're getting: the cheapest plans are storage-oriented or single-core.
  • If you need Windows, start with AccuWebHosting — comparison shopping barely exists at this price.
  • Headline prices are usually intro/first-term prices; renewal rates are often higher. We track the listed price, not the renewal fine print.
