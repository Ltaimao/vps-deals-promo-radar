Which VPS Providers Raised Prices in 2026? 6 Confirmed Hikes (and 273 Plans That Didn't Move)

Memory got expensive in 2026, and VPS bills followed — but only in specific places. The short answer first: six providers raised prices this year, each case dated below, while 273 plans at five US clouds never moved at all. Q: Why are VPS prices going up in 2026?

Memory. netcup's CEO wrote on 2026-09-22 that the company now pays about five times what it paid a year earlier for DDR4 and DDR5. OVHcloud's founder Octave Klaba warned publicly (The Register, 2026-08-11) that OVH was paying six times its June-2025 memory price by June 2026 and expected nine times by September — and announced new-server price rises of up to 87% from September. TrendForce data put Q1 2026 DRAM contract prices up 93–98% quarter over quarter. When the component that defines a VPS gets that expensive, memory-heavy plans are the first bills to move.

Q: Did every VPS plan get more expensive?

No — and this is the part most headlines skip. The same nightly tracking found 273 plans that never moved once: every active VPS and cloud plan at DigitalOcean (90 plans), Linode (39), Vultr (79), AWS Lightsail (25) and Hostwinds (40), tracked since February 2026, each with exactly one distinct price in the whole series.

The pattern of the hikes matters more than the count. Two of the six providers didn't raise any existing plan's price at all — they replaced a plan generation: netcup swapped G12 for G12.5, OVHcloud swapped its VPS 2026 range for VPS 2027. A tracker that compares tonight's price of a plan against last night's sees zero change; the increase shows up as new rows, not changed rows. If you only watch one plan's history, a generational price hike is invisible.

Q: What's still cheap after the 2026 hikes?

A hike doesn't always mean "expensive now". OVHcloud's new entry tier (VPS-1 in the 2027 range) starts at €3.81/month on a 12-month term — it's a smaller box than before (2 vCores / 4 GB), but it's cheap. netcup's VPS 500, even after +40%, still costs about a third of the same 2 vCPU / 4 GB configuration at DigitalOcean on a 12-month term. And the 273 unmoved plans mean the US clouds are exactly where they were in February.

Our take: don't read "VPS prices are up" as "everything costs more". Check the specific plan and its generation — the 2026 story is that memory-heavy plans at six providers got pricier, while hundreds of others didn't budge. Price the renewal term, not the intro banner, and you'll see which hikes actually touch your bill.
