How to compare VPS prices without fooling yourself

A downloadable table only helps if you read it right. Five rules, each one learned from our own scraper output:

  1. Check the billing unit first. Our feed mixes hourly and monthly prices in the same list — DigitalOcean, Kamatera, CloudCone and Liquid Web all have entries priced at $0.01–$0.03 that are per-second or per-hour rates, not $0.01/month plans. The CSV's plan column keeps the provider's own wording ("Hourly rate", "Per-sec billing") so you can spot them; the on-page table above skips sub-$1 rows for exactly this reason.
  2. Don't sort across currencies. 211 of our 224 rows are USD, but 7 are GBP and 6 are EUR. We deliberately left the CSV prices unconverted — a frozen exchange rate would go stale faster than the prices. Filter the currency column before you sort by price.
  3. "Starting at" is not your checkout price. Most of these rows are entry-tier anchors. Click the check link: that's the provider's own page, which is also where our scraper read the number. If the page disagrees with the row, trust the page — and note that our last_checked date tells you exactly how old the row is.
  4. Mind the date, always. Every row carries its own verification date. This snapshot was generated 2026-09-29; we re-read all 31 provider pages every 6 hours, so the next article's table will carry a newer one. A price without a date is a rumor — this table refuses to be one.
  5. Know what the table can't tell you. Honestly: we don't track renewal prices yet (providers rarely publish them cleanly), and we haven't run independent performance benchmarks. The table answers "what does it cost today" — not "what will it cost in year two" or "how fast is it". We'd rather show you the boundary than let you discover it at checkout.

The 30-second workflow

  1. Download the CSV and open it in any spreadsheet.
  2. Filter currency to one value, filter plan out of any "hourly"/"per-sec" rows if you want monthly plans only.
  3. Sort by price. Shortlist three providers.
  4. Click each provider's offer_url and confirm the price, the billing unit, and the renewal terms on their own page.
  5. Check the last_checked column — if it's older than a few days, that row is a lead, not a quote.
