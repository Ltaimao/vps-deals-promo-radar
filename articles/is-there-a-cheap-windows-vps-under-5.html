Q: Why is there no $5 Windows VPS in the mainstream market?

Because the two cheapest ingredients of a Linux VPS don't exist for Windows. Linux is free to license, and a usable Linux VPS runs on 1 GB of RAM — which is why our snapshot shows 15 providers selling entry plans at $5 or less. Windows Server is licensed per server per month from Microsoft (the SPLA model), and Windows itself needs at least 2 GB of RAM just for the OS, 4 GB for anything meaningful. Both costs land on the same bill. One provider we track, VPSMart, puts it bluntly: cheap Windows plans at $5 usually come with only 1 GB of RAM, "which is almost entirely consumed by the OS on boot."

Q: Why is a Windows VPS more expensive than Linux, exactly?

Three costs stack up. First, the license: providers pay Microsoft a per-server monthly fee for every Windows VPS they run, and that fee is embedded in the price — one Microsoft-partner host calls it "significant." Second, RAM: a minimal Linux stack (Nginx plus a small app) runs comfortably in 512 MB to 1 GB; Windows Server needs at least 2 GB for the OS alone. Third, disk: a minimal Linux install uses 2–4 GB of disk, while a Windows Server install needs 15–20 GB before you install anything. Industry guides put the typical premium at 15–25% over an equivalent Linux plan — and our own same-provider check lands right in that range: AccuWebHosting's Windows plan at $6.33 vs its Linux plan at $4.99 is a 27% premium, from the same snapshot on the same day.

Q: Can I just install Windows on a $4.99 Linux VPS myself?

The $4.99 plans are the wrong shape for it twice over. First, the specs: our snapshot's cheapest Linux tiers are 1 GB RAM boxes, and 1 GB is, as VPSMart's guide notes, "almost entirely consumed by the OS on boot" for Windows — you'd buy a server that can't run anything. Second, the license: you still need a Windows Server license for the installation; the provider's license fee doesn't transfer to your own image. A usable Windows VPS starts at 2 GB of RAM, and nothing in our 214-card snapshot pairs 2 GB with Windows below $6.33.

Q: What is the cheapest Windows VPS that actually works?

Define "works" as 2+ GB of RAM and a real Windows Server license. In the mainstream market we track, it's AccuWebHosting at $6.33/mo — the only Windows card in our snapshot. Outside our tracking, three niche options get cited with real numbers: VPSMart at $4.05/mo (2 cores, 4 GB RAM, 60 GB SSD, Windows Server 2016–2025 included), OrangeVPS at $21.50/year (~$1.79/mo, 2 vCPU/4 GB RAM, Windows available), and YottaSrc at €1.99/mo (1 core, 2 GB RAM). Treat those as outliers, not benchmarks: they're small providers with limited stock, and the 1 GB configurations (e.g. ExtraVM's $5/mo, 1 vCPU/1 GB RAM) are the unusable kind the first question warns about. If a sub-$5 Windows VPS has 1 GB of RAM, the price is the marketing — the server is a paperweight.

Q: What about Forex trading VPS? I keep seeing "Windows" there.

Forex trading software like MT4 and MT5 runs on Windows, which is why the Forex niche is the one corner of the cheap-VPS world where Windows is the default. Our snapshot carries exactly one Forex-flavored card: AccuWebHosting's "Forex Trading VPS Plan" starting at $7.99/mo. That's consistent with the general rule: the moment a workload requires Windows, the price floor jumps from ~$1 to $6–8. If your trading bot is the reason you're shopping, budget $7.99 and up, and check latency to your broker — a cheap VPS far from the broker's server costs more in slippage than it saves in rent.

Our take

The sub-$5 Windows VPS is a mirage in the mainstream market, and the one time it appears (VPSMart at $4.05, or the annual-deal outliers), it's a small-provider special, not a market price. Here's my honest read: if your workload genuinely needs Windows — MetaTrader, .NET, RDP access — budget $6.33+ and 2 GB of RAM minimum, and buy the $4.05-or-better kind of deal only if you can live with a niche provider. If the "Windows" you want is just a remote desktop you can click around in, Linux with a lightweight desktop over RDP costs nothing extra and starts at $1.00 in our data. The one purchase I wouldn't make: any Windows VPS with 1 GB of RAM. The OS eats it at boot, and you've paid $5 a month for a machine that was full before you logged in.
