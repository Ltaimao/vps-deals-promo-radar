Q1: Do cheap VPS providers include IPv6 at all?

Yes — it is one of the quietest freebies in budget hosting. Our 2026-10-13 scrape of 214 live offers across 26 providers found IPv6 mentioned on just 2 price cards, both of them SSDNodes rows ($5.50 and $9.42) carrying the text "IPv6 Included". Nobody else puts it on the card, because in 2026 it stopped being a selling point and became table stakes.

The documentation tells the real story. DigitalOcean's own docs say "enabling IPv6 on a Droplet gives you access to its 16 additional IPv6 addresses" and "IPv6 support is available for Droplets at no additional cost", in every region. Vultr's docs say "Vultr supports IPv6 for all VPS and Bare Metal instances" — you tick one checkbox at deploy time and cloud-init handles the rest. Hetzner's documentation prices IPv4 as a line item while IPv6 rides along free. Contabo's plan pages state that "dedicated IPv4 and IPv6 addresses come with every plan", free. A real-user homelab guide we found (jlemb5's RackNerd write-up) answers "Does it include IPv6?" with "up to 100 free IPv6 addresses on request in Los Angeles and France — open a support ticket after ordering and they'll allocate them." And on the extreme budget end, one provider's community review notes "IPv6 comes enabled by default" on a dirt-cheap Amsterdam VPS.

Q2: Does the cheapest tier get it too, or is it an upsell?

The cheapest tier gets it. That is the whole point of the table above: every entry in it is either the provider's own entry plan or their universal policy. DigitalOcean's $4.00/month entry — the cheapest USD row we tracked for them on 2026-10-13 — qualifies for the same 16 free IPv6 addresses as their biggest droplet. RackNerd's $2.24 entry plan, one of the cheapest in our entire snapshot, still gets up to 100 free addresses on request. Nobody gates IPv6 behind a higher tier, because addresses are not scarce the way IPv4 is.

The median cheapest entry plan across all 26 providers we tracked on 2026-10-13 was $4.54/month, with 15 providers at $5 or under. IPv6 is included free across that whole price band — it is never the reason to pay for the next plan up.

Q3: One address, or a whole block?

A block — and a comically large one. Providers typically hand you a /64, which is 264 addresses, roughly 18 quintillion. Hetzner and Contabo both allocate a full /64 per server. DigitalOcean is the conservative one, handing out 16 individual addresses per droplet. RackNerd's "up to 100 free on request" is the stingiest of the bunch and still more than any single server will ever use.

Why a block instead of one address? IPv6 was designed around whole subnets: the standard practice is one /64 per network segment, so even a single VPS gets its own range. Practically, that means you can give every container, every service, and every tunnel endpoint its own public address without ever touching NAT or asking the provider for more.

Q4: Can I skip IPv4 entirely and go IPv6-only?

Sometimes, and it is usually cheaper when you can. Hetzner's cloud lets you create a server without the Primary IPv4 at all — a community infrastructure write-up we checked prices that at about $0.60/server-month saved. The cheap-VPS community has long kicked around the "$2.50 IPv6-only sandbox" as a WireGuard endpoint or low-traffic API box.

But be honest about the trade-off: most of the internet you need to reach still requires IPv4. An IPv6-only server can talk to the IPv6 web, but not to the large IPv4-only remainder — your users, your APIs, and most package mirrors. Unless you have a specific reason (egress address rotation, a sandbox that only talks to v6 endpoints, a WireGuard peer), keep the dual stack. The IPv4 address is already bundled in the price at most budget hosts.

Q5: Do I actually need IPv6 on a cheap VPS?

Most buyers don't — yet. WireGuard listens on both families, Minecraft's Java edition speaks IPv6, and Caddy or Nginx will serve either. IPv6 matters when: your visitors arrive on IPv6-only mobile carriers (growing fast in several markets), you want separate public addresses for services without NAT gymnastics, or you are future-proofing a server you plan to keep for years.

There is also a negative test worth running. If a provider in 2026 charges extra for IPv6, or cannot answer which tier includes it, treat that as a signal about the provider, not about the protocol. The serious budget hosts all give it away free — the 2-in-214 mention rate on pricing cards is a marketing silence, not a missing feature.

Our take: check the box, then forget about it

We treat IPv6 like SSL in 2016 — hygiene, not a buying criterion. When we look at a cheap VPS plan, the questions that move money are renewal pricing, bandwidth policy, and RAM per dollar. IPv6 is a five-second check: does the cheapest tier include it free? For every provider we could verify, the answer was yes. Tick the box at deploy time, confirm with ip -6 addr after first login, and spend your attention on the numbers that actually show up on the invoice.
