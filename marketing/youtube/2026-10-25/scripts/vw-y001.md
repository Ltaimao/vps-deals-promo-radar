## Narration

A vCPU count does not tell you how much sustained compute a VPS plan allows. Before buying a shared-CPU server, check what the provider means by shared and what its fair-use policy permits.

Start with the workload. A small site with occasional bursts has different needs from a task that keeps the CPU busy continuously. Do not assume that a quiet test represents the way your service will behave all day.

Next, read the documented CPU limits. Look for sustained-use conditions, burst rules and the provider's response when a workload exceeds them. If the policy is unclear, ask support a concrete question about your intended task before paying.

Then separate the plan label from a performance claim. Shared resources, dedicated resources and the number of advertised virtual CPUs are different pieces of information. None of them, alone, proves your application's response time.

Our take: buying a suitable resource policy matters more than collecting the largest headline CPU number. Keep a small comparison sheet with the plan, policy source, workload and unanswered question.

This is a purchasing checklist, not a benchmark. We have not run a performance test for this video and do not rank providers from an untested claim.
