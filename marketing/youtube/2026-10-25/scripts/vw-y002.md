## Narration

The nearest VPS city is a reasonable starting point, but it is not proof of the best connection for your users. Network routes and the application itself also matter.

First, identify where the people using the service connect from. Do not choose a region only because it is close to the person paying the invoice.

Next, find the provider's documented test facility or looking glass for the region, if one is available. Follow its permitted use and compare from a connection representative of your audience. A result from one network is an observation, not a worldwide latency guarantee.

Record the region, test time, method and result. If you have not run a test, label the choice as untested. A polished map must not become evidence of a measurement that never happened.

Then consider the service. Static content behind a suitable delivery layer behaves differently from an application that makes repeated requests to a distant database. Look at where the dependencies live as well as the VPS.

Our take: make a small shortlist and a test plan before making a long commitment. A slightly different route or dependency can matter more than the city name on a plan card.
