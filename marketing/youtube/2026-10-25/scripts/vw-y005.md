## Narration

A plan card that says DDoS protection leaves an important question unanswered: what happens to your service when an attack exceeds the provider's policy or filtering capacity?

Start with the official coverage description. Check the protected services, relevant limits and any exclusions. Avoid assuming that a single headline covers every protocol, application or attack scenario.

Next, look for the provider's null-routing policy. If an affected address is temporarily routed away, your service can become unreachable even while the action protects the wider network. Ask how the provider communicates the event and how service is restored under its actual policy.

Then separate network mitigation from application protection. A website challenge, an application firewall and network-level filtering solve different problems. Read the product boundaries rather than combining several marketing terms into one guarantee.

Our take: choose the documented response you can plan around. Record the policy source, support contact and the operational question that matters to your service. Do not assume a cheap plan includes an unlimited incident response service.

This is a purchase and planning checklist. It does not test defenses, simulate an attack or certify a provider's security performance.
